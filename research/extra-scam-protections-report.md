> Research from the design phase (October 2026, when the app was called Phone Angel). Nothing here is built or promised; what is built is in [docs/product-brief.md](../docs/product-brief.md) and ideas are in [docs/ideas.md](../docs/ideas.md).

# Phone Angel can add scam shields safely

Phone Angel can safely add about eight more protections, but nearly all of them work by telling and guiding Mom, not by reading her phone. The best additions are: a "Check this" tool for texts and links (share sheet plus a QR scanner), a non-contact "Safe-call card" shown when a call starts, extra drift checks for Play Protect, Samsung Auto Blocker and spam settings, a guided Private DNS setup, a bundled stalkerware and risky-app list, and an opt-in call-screening mode. Most of these need no sensitive permission. The things that look attractive (reading SMS, reading OTP codes, Accessibility automation, VPN inspection, overlays, call recording) are either barred for a non-default-handler Play app or are the exact techniques banking trojans use. The biggest risk is not technical. Play's stalkerware policy allows monitoring apps only for parents tracking children and for enterprise management, and says adult-to-adult tracking is not allowed even with consent ([Play stalkerware policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10065487)). Alerting you as guardian therefore needs a conservative design (self-enrolled, visible, coarse events only) and a pre-submission check with Play. The case for building any of this is strong: among adults 60+ who lost $10,000 or more, 41% were first contacted by phone, and 33% paid in crypto, mostly at Bitcoin ATMs ([FTC Data Spotlight](https://ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/08/false-alarm-real-scam-how-scammers-are-stealing-older-adults-life-savings)). A phone app cannot see an ATM, so education and a family code word matter as much as detection.

## What Android 16 and Samsung already do, and what they leave to Phone Angel

Phone Angel should add what the platform leaves uncovered, not duplicate it. Android 16 blocks enabling "Install unknown apps" and granting accessibility access during a call with a non-contact, and Play Protect cannot be switched off mid-call ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/security/whats-new-in-android-security-privacy-2025/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/new-anti-scam-features-android-show-3556831)). No developer API exposes or customizes these blocks, and Samsung's rollout on One UI 8.x is unconfirmed by any Samsung source. The blocks also help only when the scammer is a non-contact. They do not stop AnyDesk or TeamViewer QuickSupport, which install legitimately from Play. Google's screen-share-plus-banking warning covers only participating apps (Monzo, NatWest, Revolut, Cash App, Chase), so Mom's bank may not be covered ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-expands-android-scam-protection-feature-to-chase-cash-app-in-us/)).

Samsung adds Auto Blocker, which blocks installs from outside Galaxy Store and Google Play and is on by default for devices launched with One UI 6.1.1 or later. An S23+ upgraded from older software keeps its previous setting, so check it by hand ([Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/latin_en/support/mobile-devices/protect-your-galaxy-device-with-the-new-auto-blocker-feature/)). Phone-app "Scam detection" requires One UI 8.5 or later ([Samsung US support](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10012991/)). One UI 8.5 reached the S23 series in Korea and India by May 2026, but US timing is unconfirmed ([SamMyFans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/05/21/one-ui-8-5-global-rollout-begins-for-galaxy-s23-series/)). Google Messages Scam Detection runs on-device for non-contacts and is on by default, launched in English in the US, UK and Canada ([Google blog](https://blog.google/security/new-ai-powered-scam-detection-features/)). Samsung's "Intelligence Blocking" starts with the S25 series, so the S23+ should not be assumed to have it ([Asiae](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026061609111358917)).

A third-party app can seldom read these settings. The one reliable signal is Play Protect state, which comes only through the Play Integrity API's `playProtectVerdict` (`POSSIBLE_RISK` means Play Protect is off) and requires a backend to decode ([Integrity verdicts](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/verdicts)). The practical pattern is a one-time guardian checklist ("Auto Blocker on, Scam detection on, Google Messages is the default SMS app") that you tick off while holding her phone. Phone Angel can then re-prompt monthly. The app should never claim it verified something it cannot read.

The first two features need no restricted permission and address the largest loss channels. Features 3 to 5 extend the drift monitoring you already have. Features 6 to 8 carry more policy or maintenance cost and should wait.

Data-source notes for features 1 and 4 are limited by licensing. Safe Browsing is "for non-commercial use only", which fits a free, ad-free family app, but the terms do not address Play distribution ([Safe Browsing terms](https://developers.google.com/safe-browsing/terms)). Embedding the key in an APK exposes it. VirusTotal's public API forbids commercial products and allows only 4 requests per minute ([VirusTotal docs](https://docs.virustotal.com/reference/public-vs-premium-api)), so it does not fit per-user lookups. The best design is a periodically synced local blocklist, built by a GitHub Action from Phishing.Database (MIT licence covers the repo; whether it covers the data files is unconfirmed) and URLhaus (Auth-Key mandatory since mid-2025, fair use, dumps no more often than every 5 minutes) ([abuse.ch](https://abuse.ch/blog/community-first/); [URLhaus](https://urlhaus.abuse.ch/api/)). This keeps her messages on-device and avoids rate limits. Smishing domains are often new and unlisted, so add local heuristics (punycode, brand name plus extra words, cheap TLDs, raw IPs, `@` in URLs) and present them as "be careful", not "this is a scam".

Two caveats shape feature 7. First, FTC complaint numbers are unverified self-reports and mostly spoofed, so they give a soft amber label and not a block ([FTC DNC data](https://www.ftc.gov/policy-notices/open-government/data-sets/do-not-call-data)). Behaviour-based protection (non-contact handling plus the hang-up card) beats number lists. Second, taking the screening role is itself a default change, so the drift detector must treat Phone Angel as the intended holder and alert if the role moves. A conflict-free alternative is not to take the role at all, and just to verify that Samsung Smart Call or Google spam protection stays on. I recommend that alternative for v1.

## Guardian alerts are the highest policy risk, so design them as consented sharing

Play's stalkerware policy is the one place where this project could be rejected or removed regardless of how good the engineering is. The policy summary says monitoring is allowed for apps "exclusively designed and marketed for parents to track their children or enterprise management", that other behaviour-monitoring apps are allowed only if they never hide, show a persistent notification whenever running and use a unique icon, and that apps "cannot be used to track anyone else", such as a spouse, even with knowledge or permission ([Play stalkerware announcement](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10065487)). An app that reports an adult's device events to a family member sits close to that line. The source is a 2020 summary; the current text in the Developer Program Policy was not retrieved, so what follows is a reading of the risk and not a legal or Play-confirmed conclusion.

The mitigations that most likely keep it defensible follow. Mom installs and enrolls herself, or does so with you present, and a plain-language screen names you as the recipient and shows an example alert. Alerts carry coarse security events only ("Play Protect turned off", "AnyDesk installed", "default browser changed"), never message content, call numbers, location or app-usage logs. She can preview, pause and unpair at any time from a large visible button. A persistent notification shows while sharing is active, the icon is unique, and there is no hidden mode and no remote control. Describe the feature in the listing and data-safety form as "security alert sharing" for a consented helper, and never market it as monitoring or tracking. Ship it as an off-by-default setting. Also keep a fallback: if Play objects, Phone Angel still works with local alerts on her phone plus an "ask my helper" button that she presses herself, which sends a message only when she chooses. Before building the digest in feature 8, ask Play Developer Support whether "elder-safety alert sharing with a consented family member" is acceptable and keep the reply. Because you are a solo developer, a single rejection or suspension costs the whole app, so do not make the guardian channel a load-bearing feature of the first release.

Other data-handling rules apply regardless of the alerting choice. Installed-app inventory is personal and sensitive data and requires a prominent in-app disclosure and affirmative consent shown during normal use, not only in a privacy policy ([User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)). Do not forward her call numbers in alerts. Keep alert payloads small enough that she can read them aloud to a stranger without harm.

## What not to attempt

- **Reading SMS (`READ_SMS`, `RECEIVE_SMS`) or becoming the default SMS app.** Play limits these to the default handler or approved exceptions, and "family or device locator" is listed as an invalid use. The anti-smishing exception requires "a track record of significant protection for users", which a one-family app will not have ([Play SMS policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)).
- **Reading or relaying OTP codes.** Android 15 redacts OTP content from untrusted notification listeners, and Android 16 extends redaction to the lock screen in riskier situations ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-16-sensitive-notifications-lock-screen-3501564)). Deriving it by other means is also barred.
- **AccessibilityService.** Monitoring and antivirus apps are explicitly not accessibility tools. Autonomous actions, changing settings and bypassing platform privacy controls are prohibited, and a Console declaration with video is required ([Play Accessibility policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491)). Android 16 also blocks granting it during non-contact calls.
- **Call recording, transcription or auto-hang-up.** Recording is a prohibited use of call permissions, and Accessibility cannot be used for remote call audio.
- **VpnService traffic inspection.** Allowed only for listed core functions with encryption and disclosure ([Play Permissions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)); Private DNS guidance gets most of the benefit for none of the review burden.
- **Overlays (`SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`) and killing other apps.** I found no Play text on overlays, but they are the banking-trojan technique and Google's own Integrity verdict treats overlays as risk. Warn with notifications only.
- **Root, ADB or WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS tricks.** These conflict with Device and Network Abuse rules and would fail on a stock S23+.
- **Embedding Hiya, Truecaller, Tellows or Nomorobo data.** Hiya requires a signed agreement and Truecaller's licence bars redistribution ([Hiya docs](https://developer.hiya.com/docs/guides/voice-protection/number-reputation/get-started)).
- **Fetching unknown links on the phone.** Expanding a scam link from her IP confirms a live number to the scammer; skip expansion or do it server-side.
- **Auto-opening links, background clipboard reading, uploading message bodies, or saying "this is safe".**

## Unverified items and what to test first

Several things could not be confirmed and should be tested on the actual S23+ before relying on them. These are: whether Samsung's Phone app and Smart Call coexist cleanly with a third-party screening role on One UI 8.x; whether the Android 16 in-call blocks are present on Samsung builds; whether `listHarmfulApps()` (SafetyNet Verify Apps) still returns data on Android 16, since its page says Play Integrity "offers several benefits" but gives no shutdown date ([Verify Apps](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/safetynet/verify-apps)); whether `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` and `NotificationListenerService` need specific Play declarations; Play Integrity quotas and pricing; and the Phishing.Database and abuse.ch data-file licences. Notes marked as training knowledge (shortener expansion, RDAP domain age, Chrome Android menu paths, `LinkProperties` Private DNS reading, `ACTION_DIAL` needing no `CALL_PHONE`) are unverified design assumptions, not findings. Spamhaus DBL terms could not be fetched, so treat it as unusable. Sigma-rule and AndroDR style Android detection rule sets could not be located, and ThreatFabric, Cleafy and Exodus Privacy sources were not retrieved. No free list of fake-bank apps was found.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing: Phone Angel's strongest additions are not detectors, they are well-timed interruptions. A hang-up-and-call-Kevin card at the start of a non-contact call, a one-tap "Check this" for the text she is unsure about, and a monthly settings checklist address the scams that cost older adults the most, and none needs a restricted permission. Heavy detection (screen-share awareness, banking-app correlation, number reputation) is either unavailable to a normal app, duplicated by Google and Samsung, or too weak against spoofed numbers to justify the review risk.

For a solo developer, the sequencing follows from the policy exposure. Ship features 1 to 5 first, with guardian alerts reduced to a self-initiated "ask my helper" message. Then get written clarity from Play on consented elder alert sharing before adding automatic ntfy digests. If Play answers no, nothing built earlier is wasted, because every other feature works on her phone alone.
